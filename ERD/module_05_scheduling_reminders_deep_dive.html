Appointments, bay management, service reminders, pink slip tracking, and the SMS automation engine
appointments table

Every vehicle visit starts as a booking. Currently Dhalla has no appointment system — customers call and Tinku says "yeah bring it in tomorrow morning." MechWise replaces this with a structured calendar that prevents overbooking, assigns bays and mechanics, and auto-sends confirmation/reminder messages.

Appointment status lifecycle
Booked — appointment created. Appears on calendar. 24-hour reminder scheduled. Bay and mechanic optionally pre-assigned.
Confirmed — customer confirmed via SMS reply or portal. Solid green on calendar. Staff can proceed with confidence.
In progress — vehicle has arrived and a job card has been created from this appointment. Appointment is now linked to JC-xxxx. Status auto-set when job card is created.
Completed — linked job card is completed. Appointment archived.
No show — customer didn't arrive and didn't call. Auto-set if no job card created by end of booking_date. Flag on client record for future reference: "1 previous no-show."
Cancelled — customer cancelled before arriving. Reason field optional. Bay and mechanic freed up. Cancellation notification to staff.
Rescheduled — moved to a new date/time. Old appointment marked "Rescheduled → APT-xxxx". New appointment created with link to original.
Capacity calculation
Available capacity per day = (number of bays) × (operating hours). Dhalla has 4 bays × 9.5 hours (7:30am–5:00pm) = 38 bay-hours per day. Each appointment consumes estimated_duration_hrs bay-hours. When total booked hours reaches 80% of capacity, the calendar shows "Nearly full" in orange. At 100%, no more bookings accepted for that day.
Buffer time: Add a configurable gap between appointments (default 15 minutes) for vehicle handover, cleaning, and moving cars. A 1-hour minor service actually blocks 1.25 hours in the schedule.
Edge cases
Walk-ins: Customer appears without a booking. Create an appointment with source = "Walk-in", booking_date = today, booking_time = now, status = "In progress" immediately. System checks bay availability — if full, show estimated wait time: "All bays occupied. Estimated wait: 45 minutes."
Multi-day jobs: A clutch replacement takes 4+ hours — might span two days. The appointment blocks estimated_duration across both days. Bay remains assigned. Calendar shows a spanning bar across Mon-Tue.
Repeat bookings: Fleet customer wants all 5 vehicles serviced one per week. Support a "Recurring booking" option that auto-creates the next appointment when the current one completes.

Business rules
The floor board auto-refreshes every 60 seconds (or on any status change). It's the primary operational view for the workshop owner — a single glance shows what's happening across all bays.
When a "Ready for pickup" vehicle hasn't been collected within 2 hours, the bay turns yellow with "Awaiting collection — consider moving to yard" — freeing the bay for the next job.
Job type → bay type matching: Pink slip inspections need a hoist or pit. Tyre work needs the tyre bay. The system should suggest the appropriate bay when creating a booking, but allow override (small workshop, flexible setup).
Edge cases
More jobs than bays: 6 appointments today but only 4 bays. The scheduler should show "Overbooking warning: 2 jobs may need to queue." Front desk manages by staggering start times — 8:00am, 8:30am, 9:00am — so bays free up as minor services complete.
Vehicle shuffle: Bay 2 is blocked waiting for parts, but another vehicle needs a hoist. The system should support "Move to yard" on the blocked vehicle, freeing Bay 2 and logging the move.
service_reminders table

The retention engine. Every completed invoice auto-creates a reminder for the next service. The system then auto-sends SMS/email at configurable intervals before the due date. This is the single biggest revenue driver for workshops — reminders bring customers back without the owner making a single phone call.

Reminder status lifecycle
Pending — reminder created, no messages sent yet. Waiting for the first send window (typically 30 days before due_date).
Sent — at least one reminder message has been sent. System continues sending at intervals until customer responds or reminder expires.
Booked — customer booked an appointment in response. Reminder stops sending. appointment_id linked.
Completed — service has been done. New reminder auto-created from the new invoice. Lifecycle restarts.
Declined — customer said "not interested" or "going elsewhere." Stop sending. Flag on client record for awareness but no further action.
Expired — due_date passed by 90+ days with no response or booking. Auto-archived. System stops sending. Vehicle may have been sold or customer has moved on.
Reminder send cadence (configurable)
30 days before — first reminder. Friendly tone: "Hi Peter, your Hyundai Tucson NSW-PL1 is due for service on 7 Apr. Book online or reply to this message."
14 days before — second reminder (only if no response). Slightly more direct: "Reminder: service due in 2 weeks for your Tucson. Call us on 0247 082 717 or reply BOOK."
7 days before — final reminder (only if no response). Urgency: "Your Tucson service is due next week. Don't miss it — book now to avoid delays."
Due date passes — one overdue notice (only if no response). "Your service was due on 7 Apr. It's not too late — call us to book."
30 days overdue — final follow-up. After this, reminders stop automatically.
Business rules
When a customer replies "BOOK" or "YES" to a reminder SMS, the system creates a draft appointment (status: Booked, source: SMS Reply) and sends: "Great! We've pencilled you in. We'll call to confirm a time. — Dhalla Automotive"
When a customer replies "STOP" or "NO", set status to Declined, client_response = "Not Interested". Add a note to the client record. Remove from future reminder sends but don't deactivate — they may come back for a different service.
Duplicate prevention: If a vehicle already has a "Pending" or "Sent" reminder of the same type, don't create another. When a new invoice generates a new reminder, it supersedes the old one (old set to Completed, new set to Pending).
Current data: 212 service + 64 pink slip reminders migrated
These are the reminders already imported from the Dhalla database. Many have due dates in the past (overdue). On first launch, the system should batch-process these: auto-send an SMS to all overdue reminders within the last 90 days, skip anything older (likely lost customers).
Edge cases
No phone or email: 88 of 300 clients have no phone number. These reminders can't be sent automatically. Flag them as "Manual follow-up needed" on the dashboard. As contact details are captured through progressive data entry, the reminders become sendable.
Vehicle sold: If a vehicle ownership changes (client_vehicles.ownership_end is set), cancel all pending reminders for the old owner and create new ones for the new owner.
Pink slip (e-safety) tracking

NSW requires an annual safety inspection ("pink slip" or e-safety check) for vehicles over 5 years old. This is a revenue opportunity — every 12 months, every older car needs one, and the workshop earns $52 per inspection plus any repair work discovered. Dhalla already has 79 pink slip records with expiry dates.

How pink slips work in NSW
1. Vehicle owner brings car for inspection. Workshop must hold an MVRL (Motor Vehicle Repairer Licence) to conduct e-safety checks.
2. Mechanic inspects against the RMS checklist: brakes, steering, suspension, tyres, lights, wipers, exhaust, body condition, windscreen.
3. If it passes: e-safety certificate issued via the DRIVES system (NSW government portal). Certificate number recorded.
4. If it fails: defect report issued listing what needs fixing. Customer must fix and return for re-inspection.
5. Certificate is valid for 6 months (for registration renewal purposes).

Pink slip reminder cadence
60 days before expiry — "Hi [name], your pink slip for [rego] expires on [date]. Book your e-safety check early to avoid delays. Call us or reply BOOK."
30 days before — "Reminder: [rego] pink slip expires in 30 days. You'll need a valid inspection to renew your registration."
14 days before — "Urgent: [rego] pink slip expires on [date]. Book now to ensure you can renew your rego on time."
Expiry day — "Your pink slip for [rego] has expired today. You cannot renew registration without a valid inspection. Call us on [phone]."
Business rules
Pink slip reminders start earlier (60 days) than service reminders (30 days) because registration renewal deadlines are hard — missing a pink slip means the car can't be legally driven.
Only MVRL-certified staff (checked via staff.certifications) can be assigned to pink slip job cards. The system should enforce this — if a non-MVRL mechanic tries to complete a pink slip job, show: "Only MVRL-certified staff can sign off e-safety inspections."
Pink slip fail → repair → re-inspection is a two-visit workflow. The first visit generates a job card for the inspection ($52) plus a quote for repairs. After repairs, a second job card is created for the re-inspection (often free or reduced fee). The system should link these: "Re-inspection for JC-0045."
Edge cases
New vehicles: Cars under 5 years old don't need a pink slip in NSW (they have a "blue slip" exemption for new registrations). The system should suppress pink slip reminders for vehicles where vehicle_year is within the last 5 years, unless manually flagged.
Interstate vehicles: VIC has a "roadworthy certificate," QLD has a "safety certificate." Different states, different requirements. The vehicle.state field determines which inspection regime applies. Phase 1 focuses on NSW pink slips; phase 2 adds state-specific logic.

SMS provider integration
Phase 1: Integrate with an Australian SMS gateway — MessageMedia, Sinch, or Twilio. Cost is approximately $0.06–$0.08 per SMS. At ~500 messages/month (reminders + confirmations + pickups), that's ~$35/month — easily justified by the extra bookings it generates.
Sender ID: Messages appear from "Dhalla Auto" (or workshop name, max 11 chars) rather than a random phone number. Customer replies go to a virtual number that the system monitors for keywords: YES, NO, BOOK, STOP, C (confirm), R (reschedule).
Two-way SMS: When the customer replies, the system parses the response. "YES" or "BOOK" → create appointment. "NO" or "STOP" → decline and cease sends. "C" → confirm booking. "R" → flag for front desk to call back. Anything else → forward to front desk as an unrecognised reply to handle manually.
SMS compliance (Australian regulations)
ACMA Spam Act 2003: Every promotional SMS must include an opt-out mechanism. Service reminders are transactional (existing customer relationship), so they're exempt from some provisions, but customers must still be able to stop receiving them. Every message includes: "Reply STOP to opt out."
Send window: SMS must be sent between 9:00am and 8:00pm local time (AEST/AEDT). The system uses the workshop.timezone field to enforce this. A reminder scheduled for 8:30pm is held until 9:00am the next morning.
Frequency cap: No more than 1 SMS per customer per week (excluding direct replies to customer messages). Prevents annoyance. If a service reminder and a pink slip reminder are both due in the same week, combine them into one message.
SMS audit log
Every SMS sent is logged: timestamp, recipient phone, message text, delivery status (sent, delivered, failed, bounced), cost, related entity (reminder_id, appointment_id, quote_id). Monthly SMS cost shown on the dashboard.
Edge cases
Invalid phone numbers: Some migrated numbers are malformed (11 digits, 9 digits, missing leading 0). The SMS gateway will reject these. The system should pre-validate before sending and flag: "SMS to Peter failed — invalid number 04690813555. Please update."
Customer with no phone but has email: Fall back to email for all reminder types. If neither phone nor email exists, flag as "No contact method — manual follow-up required."
