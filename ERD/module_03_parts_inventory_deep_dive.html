Business rules
Categories drive the inventory filter on the stock screen. When the mechanic is looking for an oil filter, they click "Filters" and see only PC-02 items — not all 123.
The current Dhalla system mixes physical parts and service bundles in the same list (e.g. "REPLACED ENGINE OIL 10W/40 PENRITE SAMI SYNTHETIC OIL & OIL FILTER & ENGINE FLUSH & 25 POINT CHECK" at $154 is an entire minor service listed as an "item"). In MechWise, service bundles are parent jobs in the catalogue — only physical parts go in inventory. This distinction is enforced by the category structure.
Edge cases
Uncategorised items: When importing from the Dhalla system, some items don't clearly fit a category (e.g. "R2605", "R2606" — these are part numbers without descriptions). The system should have a "Needs review" auto-flag for items imported without a category, shown as a task on the dashboard.
parts table

Every physical item the workshop buys, stocks, uses, and sells. This is the largest and most field-heavy table in the system — it tracks what you have, what it's worth, where it is, who supplies it, and when to reorder.

Markup = (sell − cost) ÷ cost. Margin = (sell − cost) ÷ sell. The workshop owner cares about margin — it's the profit percentage on each sale.
Business rules
cost_price_ex_gst is never shown to mechanics or front desk — only visible to Owner and Manager roles. This prevents staff from seeing supplier pricing.
When sell_price_ex_gst is changed, the system recalculates sell_price_inc_gst immediately. It also warns if any job sub-items are linked to this part: "This price change will affect 3 job sub-items. Update their standard prices too?"
Negative margin alert: If cost_price exceeds sell_price (e.g. supplier raised prices but sell price wasn't updated), the system shows a red warning on the part record and in the dashboard: "5 parts are currently selling below cost."
Edge cases
Service items masquerading as parts: The Dhalla system has items like "Replace Coolant" at $20 and "Service" at $154. These are services, not physical stock items. During migration, flag these as "Service bundle — move to jobs catalogue" and exclude from inventory.
Variable pricing by vehicle: A timing belt for a Subaru might cost $80 from the supplier vs $45 for a Toyota. The parts table stores the default cost/sell, but the system should allow a price override when the part is added to a specific job card. The override doesn't change the catalogue — just that job.
Pack sizes vs unit pricing: Spark plugs come in boxes of 4 but are used individually. The unit_of_measure should be "Each" with qty tracked per plug, even though the purchase order quantity is in multiples of 4. PO lines handle the pack-to-unit conversion.
Stock lifecycle

How qty_in_stock changes over time. There are exactly five events that move stock levels — two that add, two that subtract, and one that corrects.

Stock movements
EventTriggerEffect on qty_in_stock
PO receivedPurchase order status changes to "Received" or "Partially received"+ qty_received from PO line. e.g. received 10 oil filters → stock goes from 2 to 12.
Return to stockPart removed from a job card before completion, or job card cancelled+ qty that was reserved. Mechanic pulled a wrong filter, put it back.
Job card completedJob card status changes to "Completed" and parts are confirmed as used− qty from each job card part line. 1 oil filter used → stock drops by 1.
Wastage/damageManual stock adjustment with reason "Damaged", "Expired", or "Lost"− qty written off. Requires a reason field and manager approval.
Stocktake adjustmentPhysical count differs from system count during stocktakeSet to actual counted quantity. Difference logged as variance. e.g. system says 8 filters, physical count is 6 → adjust to 6, log -2 variance.
Stock movement audit log
Every stock change writes a row to a stock_movements audit table: part_id, movement_type (received, used, adjusted, wasted), qty_change (+/-), previous_qty, new_qty, related_doc (PO-0001 or JC-0015), performed_by (staff_id), timestamp, reason.
This audit trail is immutable — no edits, no deletes. It provides a complete history of where every unit of stock came from and where it went. Essential for shrinkage detection and supplier dispute resolution.
Stocktake workflow
1. Owner triggers "Start stocktake" from inventory screen. System generates a printable stocktake sheet grouped by location (Shelf A1, Shelf B1, Tyre rack, etc.).
2. Staff walks through the workshop counting physical items, writes the actual count on the sheet (or enters directly on a tablet).
3. System compares actual vs system counts. Highlights discrepancies in red. Shows total variance value (e.g. "12 items differ, total variance value: -$234").
4. Owner reviews and approves adjustments. Each adjustment requires a reason: "Damaged", "Used but not logged", "Supplier short-delivered", or "Count error".
5. System updates all qty_in_stock values and writes audit records. Dashboard shows "Last stocktake: 15 Jun 2025" and alerts if overdue (configurable: monthly or quarterly).
Edge cases
Bulk fluids: Engine oil is bought in 20L drums but dispensed in litres. Track in litres as unit_of_measure. When a PO for "1 × 20L drum" is received, stock increases by 20 litres. When a minor service uses 5L, stock decreases by 5. The PO line needs a unit conversion field: "1 drum = 20 litres".
Serialised high-value parts: Batteries and alternators might need individual serial numbers for warranty tracking. Phase 1 doesn't need full serial tracking, but the notes field on the job card line should capture the serial/batch number of the specific unit installed.
Reorder engine

The logic that tells the workshop owner "you're running low on oil filters — order more." The current Dhalla system has basic alerts (red flag when stock hits the alert number). MechWise makes this smarter with lead-time awareness, suggested PO generation, and usage-rate forecasting.

Smart reorder (phase 2 enhancement)
Usage-rate calculation: Track how many units of each part are consumed per month (from job card completions). If oil filters average 15/month and current stock is 18 with lead time of 2 days, the system knows stock will last ~5 weeks — no urgency. But if lead time is 7 days and stock is 5 (2-week supply), trigger the alert earlier.
Seasonal adjustment: AC gas usage spikes in summer (Nov-Feb in Australia). If the system sees a 3× usage rate for AC-related parts in December, it should adjust reorder timing accordingly. This is a phase 2 ML feature — phase 1 uses fixed reorder levels.
Edge cases
Supplier out of stock: The PO is created but the supplier can't deliver for 2 weeks. The system should support a "Backorder" status on PO lines and show affected job cards that are waiting for this part.
Price change on reorder: The supplier raised the oil price from $38 to $42. The PO line should show the new cost, and the system should prompt: "Cost price has increased by 11%. Update sell price from $50 to $55 to maintain margin?"

Business rules
Suggested PO generation: When a reorder alert fires, the system auto-creates a draft PO to the primary supplier with the part, reorder_qty, and current cost price pre-filled. Owner reviews, adjusts, and sends.
Multi-part PO: If 4 parts from the same supplier hit reorder level in the same week, combine them into a single draft PO. Reduces ordering overhead and may unlock volume discounts.
Receipt price check: When receiving a PO, if the actual cost differs from the PO line cost, the system prompts: "CoolDrive charged $12 for Z432, PO says $10. Accept new price and update catalogue cost?" This keeps cost prices current without manual maintenance.
Edge cases
Supplier substitution: Ordered Z432 oil filter but supplier sends Z436 as a compatible substitute. The system should allow receiving a different part_id against a PO line with a "Substituted" flag and notes field.
Damaged on delivery: 10 filters ordered, 2 arrive damaged. Receive 8, log 2 as "Damaged on delivery" with a claim reference. PO stays at "Partially received" until the supplier sends replacements or issues a credit.

Barcode scanning workflow
1. Workshop owner prints barcode labels from the inventory screen (using a standard thermal label printer). Each label shows: part number, item name (shortened), location, and barcode.
2. Labels are stuck to the shelf edge next to each item.
3. When the mechanic needs to log a part used on a job: open job card on tablet → tap "Add part" → scan the barcode with the tablet camera → part is added to the job card with correct price and qty = 1.
4. During stocktake: scan each item, enter actual count. Much faster than scrolling through a list of 123 items.
Barcode format
Use Code 128 barcode format — it's compact, supports alphanumeric, and every scanner reads it. The barcode_sku field stores the encoded value, which can be the part_number (e.g. "Z432") or an internal SKU (e.g. "PT-008"). Generate the barcode image server-side and embed it in the printable label.
Phase 1 vs Phase 2
Phase 1: Location is a free-text field on the part record. Display it next to the part name on job card screens so mechanics can find stock. No barcode hardware required.
Phase 2: Barcode scanner integration. Print labels, scan to add parts to job cards, scan during stocktake. Requires a thermal label printer (~$150) and using the tablet camera as a scanner (no dedicated hardware needed).
Edge cases
Multiple locations: A popular oil filter might be stocked on Shelf B1 (primary) and also kept in a bulk storage area. Support a primary_location and a notes field for overflow locations. The display shows the primary location.
Location changes: When the workshop reorganises shelving, they need to bulk-update locations. A "Relocate" function in the inventory screen lets the owner select all parts on "Shelf A1" and move them to "Shelf A2" in one action.
