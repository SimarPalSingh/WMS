job_categories table

The top-level grouping. Every service the workshop offers falls into one of these buckets. Categories organise the job picker in quotes and job cards — the mechanic selects a category first, then drills into specific jobs.

Business rules
Categories cannot be deleted if they have active jobs linked to them. The owner must first move or deactivate all child jobs before deactivating a category.
The display_order is drag-and-drop reorderable in the settings page. The most-used category at this workshop is "Engine service" (minor/major services make up roughly 60% of all jobs), so it defaults to position 1.
Categories are workshop-level, not global. When you sell to a second workshop that specialises in 4WDs, they might want different categories like "Winch and recovery", "Diff locks", "Snorkel and air intake". The system should allow custom categories per workshop.
Edge cases
A job that spans two categories: replacing a timing belt (CAT-01 engine) might also require a water pump (CAT-04 cooling). The job belongs to the primary category — the parts and sub-items can reference any category. Don't force a 1:1 mapping.
jobs table (parent jobs)

A parent job is what the customer sees on their quote: "Minor service — $154". Behind it sits a list of sub-items that define exactly what work is done, what parts are used, and how long it takes. The parent job is the package; the sub-items are the components.

Business rules
standard_total_price auto-recalculates whenever a sub-item's price changes. The owner can also hard-set an override price that "locks" the total (e.g. always quote minor service at $154 even if sub-item costs drift). When locked, a icon appears next to the price.
estimated_duration is the sum of sub-item labour times but can be manually adjusted. A major service on a BMW 5 Series takes longer than on a Toyota Corolla — the system should support vehicle-make-specific duration overrides in v2.
The original Dhalla system had 58 flat job names mixing parent-level items ("Major Service") with sub-items ("Oil Filter", "Spark Plug"). In MechWise, these are properly nested. The 58 items have been restructured into 25 parent jobs containing 40+ sub-items.
Edge cases
Ad-hoc jobs: Sometimes the mechanic discovers extra work mid-job ("found a leaking radiator hose during the minor service"). The system needs an "Add line item" function on the job card that doesn't require creating a new parent job. These ad-hoc items belong to the "General labour" category with a custom description and price.
Vehicle-specific pricing: A clutch replacement on a Subaru WRX costs more than on a Hyundai i30 (different labour hours, different parts). Phase 1 handles this by overriding the price per quote. Phase 2 should support vehicle-make pricing tables: "Clutch kit — Subaru: $1,200, Hyundai: $800, Default: $950".
job_sub_items table

The atomic unit of work. Each sub-item answers three questions: what part does it use, how long does it take, and what does it cost? This is the pricing engine of the entire platform — every quote, job card, and invoice is built from sub-items.

FieldTypeTagsValidation and notes
sub_item_idVARCHAR(10)PKAutoFormat: JS-001. Sequential.
sub_item_nameVARCHAR(150)RequiredDescriptive name shown on invoices. e.g. "Engine oil change (10W-40 semi-synthetic 5L)". Include enough detail that the customer knows what they got.
job_idVARCHAR(10)FKRequiredWhich parent job this belongs to. A sub-item always belongs to exactly one parent. If the same task appears in multiple parent jobs (e.g. "25-point check" in both minor and major service), create separate sub-item records for each.
category_idVARCHAR(10)FKInherited from parent job but can be overridden. Useful when a sub-item crosses categories (e.g. water pump replacement under a timing belt job).
part_costDECIMAL(10,2)RequiredThe sell price of parts consumed by this sub-item. Set to 0 for labour-only items (e.g. "25-point check"). Can link to a specific part_id for auto-pricing — see the "Job ↔ parts" tab.
labour_time_hrsDECIMAL(4,2)RequiredValidatedEstimated time in decimal hours. 0.25 = 15 min, 0.5 = 30 min, 1.0 = 60 min. Must be ≥ 0. Used for scheduling, time tracking, and labour cost calculation.
labour_rate_hrDECIMAL(8,2)The rate charged to the customer per hour. Defaults to the workshop's standard rate (typically $80-$120/hr in Western Sydney). Can be overridden per sub-item for specialist work (e.g. diagnostic at $100/hr).
labour_costDECIMAL(10,2)Calculated= labour_time_hrs × labour_rate_hr. Auto-calculated, not manually entered. e.g. 0.3 hrs × $80/hr = $24.
standard_priceDECIMAL(10,2)Calculated= part_cost + labour_cost. This is the line item total on a quote. e.g. $50 (oil) + $24 (labour) = $74.
is_activeBOOLEANInactive sub-items are excluded from future quotes but remain on historical records.

Business rules
When the workshop owner changes the labour_rate_hr in workshop settings (e.g. from $80 to $90), all sub-items should recalculate. Prompt: "Update labour rate to $90/hr across all jobs? This will change prices on future quotes but not existing ones."
Part_cost can be manually set OR auto-linked to a part in inventory. When linked, the part_cost updates automatically if the sell price of that part changes. See the "Job ↔ parts" tab for details.
Sub-items with labour_time = 0 and part_cost = 0 are valid — they represent included items (e.g. "Complimentary vehicle wash" or "Courtesy check"). They appear on the invoice at $0.00 to show the customer they got something extra.
Edge cases
Variable-quantity parts: An oil change uses 1 × 5L bottle on a 4-cylinder but 2 × 5L on a V8. The sub-item has a default quantity of 1, but the mechanic should be able to adjust the quantity when building the job card. The price recalculates: 2 × $50 = $100 part cost instead of $50.
Sub-items shared across parent jobs: "Oil filter replacement" exists under both minor and major service. These are deliberately separate records (JS-002 under JB-001, JS-006 under JB-002) even though the name is identical. This avoids cascade issues — changing the minor service oil filter shouldn't affect the major service.
Flat-rate vs time-and-materials: Some workshops quote flat-rate (fixed price per job regardless of actual time), others bill by actual hours. MechWise supports both — standard_price is the flat rate; if the workshop prefers T&M billing, the job card tracks actual_labour_hrs and recalculates at invoice time.

GST calculation rules (Australian Tax Office requirements)
All prices in the catalogue are stored ex-GST (exclusive of GST). GST is calculated at the line-item level on quotes and invoices: line_total_ex_gst × 0.10 = gst_amount. The customer sees the inc-GST total. This is the correct approach for BAS reporting.
Tax type options: "GST inclusive" (default — most workshops), "GST exclusive" (for business-to-business), "GST free" (for exported services or GST-exempt items). Set per invoice, not per line item.
Some items are GST-free: certain insurance repair work, some government fleet contracts. The system should allow a GST override per line item (0% instead of 10%) with a mandatory reason field.
Discount logic
Discount types: "Percentage" (10% off total), "Fixed amount" ($50 off), "None". Applied at the quote/invoice header level, not per line item. Discount reduces the ex-GST subtotal before GST is calculated.
Maximum discount without owner approval: configurable in settings. Default 15%. If front desk tries to apply a 20% discount, the system requires owner PIN or approval notification.
Discounts must never take a line item below cost. The system should warn: "This discount takes the total below cost price. Margin: -$23. Apply anyway?" Only the owner can override.
Margin visibility
For users with can_view_financials = TRUE, every quote and invoice shows a hidden margin row: (sell price − cost price) ÷ sell price = margin %. This helps the owner make pricing decisions without switching to a separate report.
Edge cases
Insurance jobs: The insurer has a pre-agreed rate schedule that may differ from the workshop's standard pricing. Support an "Insurance rate" price list — when the job card is flagged as "Insurance claim", prices auto-switch to the insurer's schedule.
Warranty work: If a part fails within warranty period (e.g. 12 months or 20,000km), the replacement labour and parts should be at $0 to the customer but still tracked for supplier warranty claims. The system needs a "Warranty" line type that records the normal cost internally but shows $0.00 on the customer invoice.
job_item_parts table (new linking table)

This is the glue between the service catalogue and inventory. It answers: "When we do this sub-item, which parts do we typically use?" This enables two critical automations: auto-populating the parts list on a job card, and auto-deducting stock when a job is completed.

FieldTypeTagsValidation and notes
link_idVARCHAR(10)PKAutoFormat: JPL-001.
sub_item_idVARCHAR(10)FKRequiredWhich job sub-item this part is for.
part_idVARCHAR(10)FKRequiredWhich part from inventory. e.g. PT-001 "Penrite 10W-40 5L".
default_qtyINTEGERRequiredHow many units are typically used. e.g. 1 oil filter, 4 spark plugs, 2 brake pads. Mechanic can adjust on the actual job card.
is_requiredBOOLEANTRUE = always included (e.g. oil filter in an oil change). FALSE = optional/conditional (e.g. cabin filter may be skipped if recently replaced).
notesTEXTe.g. "Use Z432 for Toyota, Z436 for Mazda" or "Check VIN for correct spec".

When a parent job is selected on a new quote, the system pre-populates all sub-items and their linked parts. The mechanic reviews and adjusts (swap Z432 filter for Z436 based on the vehicle, change oil quantity from 5L to 6L for a larger engine).
When a job card is marked "Completed", every linked part with is_required = TRUE auto-deducts from parts.qty_in_stock. If stock would go negative, the system warns: "Oil filter Z432 is out of stock. 0 remaining."
Multiple compatible parts: A sub-item like "Oil filter replacement" might accept Z432 OR Z436 OR Z456 depending on the vehicle. The link table supports multiple part options per sub-item — the mechanic picks the right one, or the system auto-suggests based on vehicle make if that mapping exists.

Edge cases
Customer-supplied parts: The customer brings their own oil or filter. The sub-item still appears on the job card (for labour tracking) but part_cost is set to $0 and no stock is deducted. The "Owner parts — labour only" parent job (JB-021) handles this scenario.
Parts not in inventory: A one-off special-order part (e.g. a specific gasket for a rare vehicle). The mechanic should be able to add an ad-hoc line item with a manual description and price, without creating a permanent inventory record. Flag it as "Non-stock item".

What happens when this is quoted
1. Front desk selects client (CL-005: Peter), vehicle (VH-001: NSW-PL1 Hyundai Tucson), and job (JB-001: Minor service).
2. System auto-loads 4 sub-items with linked parts. Front desk sees the oil filter has 3 options — selects Z456 (Hyundai-specific).
3. Quote total shows $154.00 (package price). System auto-calculates GST: $154 × 10% = $15.40 → Total inc GST: $169.40.
4. Quote is sent to Peter via SMS. He replies "YES". Status changes to "Approved". System auto-creates a job card for the next available slot.
What happens when this is completed
5. Mechanic marks each sub-item as done. Actual labour logged: 0.9 hrs (faster than estimated 1.0 hr — good efficiency).
6. On "Complete job", 3 parts auto-deduct from inventory: PT-001 (1 unit), PT-010 Z456 (1 unit), PT-029 flush (1 unit). Z456 drops to 4 remaining (above reorder level of 5? triggers alert).
7. Invoice auto-generates from job card lines. PDF generated with ABN, GST breakdown, vehicle details. Emailed to Peter (if email captured) or printed.
8. Next service due auto-set: current date + 6 months = 2026-04-02, current mileage 92,500 + 10,000 = 102,500 km. Service reminder created in reminders table.
