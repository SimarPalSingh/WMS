Multi-tenancy architecture

MechWise is designed to sell to many workshops, each operating independently on the same platform. Every row of data belongs to a workshop_id. Workshop A never sees Workshop B's clients, vehicles, invoices, or financials. This is the architectural decision that makes the product a SaaS business instead of a one-off build.

Tenancy model: shared database, logical isolation
Approach: Single database, all workshops share the same tables, every table includes a workshop_id foreign key. Queries are always filtered by workshop_id. This is the most cost-effective model for a startup SaaS — one database to manage, one deployment to maintain, scales to hundreds of workshops.
Alternative considered and rejected: Separate database per workshop. More isolation, but drastically increases operational cost — every schema migration, every backup, every monitoring alert multiplies by the number of clients. Only justified at enterprise scale (100+ workshops).
Isolation enforcement
LayerHow isolation works
DatabaseEvery table with business data has a workshop_id column. Row-Level Security (RLS) in PostgreSQL ensures queries always filter by the authenticated user's workshop. Even a SQL injection can't read another workshop's data.
API layerEvery API request includes the workshop context from the JWT token. The backend middleware injects workshop_id into all queries automatically — developers can't accidentally forget the filter.
UI layerThe frontend receives only data for the logged-in workshop. Dropdown selectors (clients, vehicles, staff, parts) are pre-filtered. No cross-workshop data is ever loaded into the browser.
File storageInvoice PDFs, logo images, and file uploads are stored in workshop-prefixed S3 paths: /ws-001/invoices/, /ws-002/invoices/. IAM policies prevent cross-workshop access.
Tables that are workshop-scoped (have workshop_id)

Multi-workshop ownership
A single owner can manage multiple workshops under one login. e.g. "Dhalla Automotive — Kingswood" (WS-001) and "Dhalla Automotive — Penrith" (WS-002). The owner sees a workshop switcher in the top nav. Data is completely separate — different staff, different clients, different inventory — but the owner can view combined reports across both locations.
Cross-workshop features (phase 2): Transfer a vehicle between locations. Share a client record across workshops (same fleet customer uses both locations). Consolidated BAS reporting across all workshops under one ABN.
Edge cases
Staff working at multiple locations: A mechanic who splits time between Kingswood and Penrith. Create two staff records (one per workshop) or support a shared staff model with location assignments per day. Phase 1: two records. Phase 2: shared staff with scheduling.
Demo/sandbox workshop: For sales demos and training. Create a WS-DEMO workspace with pre-loaded sample data that resets nightly. Prospective customers can explore the platform without signing up.
Onboarding wizard

The first 30 minutes after a workshop signs up determines whether they stick or churn. The onboarding wizard guides the owner through the minimum setup needed to start working — not a 50-field form, but a focused sequence that gets them to "first invoice" as fast as possible.

Business rules
Every step is skippable except step 1 (business name and phone). The wizard saves progress — the owner can close the browser and resume where they left off. A "Setup checklist" widget on the dashboard shows remaining steps until fully configured.
The default job catalogue (25 parent jobs, 40+ sub-items) is pre-loaded for every new workshop. The owner ticks which ones they offer and adjusts prices to their rates. This means the workshop can start quoting and invoicing immediately without building a catalogue from scratch.
A "Getting started" email sequence (day 1, day 3, day 7, day 14) sends tips: "Did you know you can auto-send service reminders?", "Set up your SMS templates to save time", "Import your customer list to get started faster."

Business rules
Settings changes take effect immediately for new records. Existing quotes, invoices, and job cards are not retroactively affected — they retain the settings that were in place when they were created. e.g. changing the labour rate from $80 to $90 only affects future quotes.
Only Owner and Manager roles can access settings. Mechanics and front desk have no access to configuration.
SMS template customisation: The owner can edit the text of each message type but cannot remove mandatory elements (opt-out text, workshop name, rego). The system validates that template variables ({client_name}, {rego}, etc.) are present — a template missing {rego} shows a warning: "This template won't include the vehicle registration."

Business rules
14-day free trial with full Professional features. No credit card required. At day 10: email reminder "Your trial ends in 4 days." At day 14: downgrade to read-only mode. Data is preserved for 30 days — the owner can subscribe at any point to restore access.
Downgrade path: Enterprise → Professional removes multi-workshop access (data preserved but hidden). Professional → Starter removes analytics, portal, and floor board. Core workflow (quotes, invoices, payments) is always available — never lock the owner out of their money.
Cancellation: The owner keeps read-only access for 30 days after cancellation. Data export (full CSV dump) is available during this period. After 30 days, data is archived (retained for 12 months per Australian contract law) then permanently deleted.
Data migration

Every new workshop customer has existing data — spreadsheets, old software exports, paper records. The migration tool gets their data into MechWise cleanly. This is the service you charge $500–$1,500 for, and it's what prevents the customer from churning in the first week ("all my old data is gone").

White-label and branding

Every workshop that uses MechWise should feel like the system is "theirs" — their logo on the login page, their branding on invoices, their name in SMS messages. The platform infrastructure is invisible to the end customer. This is what makes the product feel professional rather than like a generic tool.

Enterprise white-label (add-on)
For Enterprise tier customers who want zero MechWise branding: custom domain (software.dhallaautomotive.com.au), custom email sender domain (noreply@dhallaautomotive.com.au), no "Powered by MechWise" footer. The customer's clients never know MechWise exists.
Custom domain requires DNS CNAME setup. MechWise provides instructions: "Point software.yourdomain.com.au to cname.mechwise.com.au." SSL certificate auto-provisioned via Let's Encrypt.
Reseller model (future)
Automotive industry consultants or franchise groups could resell MechWise under their own brand. e.g. "Autofix Pro — Powered by MechWise" with the reseller earning 20-30% of subscription revenue. This requires a reseller admin panel (manage workshops, view aggregate metrics, custom pricing). Phase 3 feature.
